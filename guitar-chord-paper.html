PUT IT TO USE

A good place to start.

  1. Write the chord name above a box.
  2. Add dots and finger numbers between the fret lines.
  3. Mark open or muted strings and note a starting fret if needed.

THE DETAIL THAT MATTERS

Label the starting fret for movable shapes.

The heavy top line can represent the nut for an open-position chord. For a higher-position diagram, write the starting fret beside the box so the shape is unambiguous.

Pick the right paper.

Choose A4 for 210 × 297 mm, or US Letter for 8.5 × 11 inches. Match the PDF and your printer’s loaded paper.

02

Select Actual size / 100% in your PDF viewer. Turn off “Fit” and “Shrink oversized pages” to preserve the spacing.

Measure the 50 mm line at the bottom of your print. If it is off, check scaling and your printer before using the grid for measurements.

A FEW PAPER NOTES

About this
useful page.

Which side is the low E string?+

In a standard front-view diagram, the leftmost vertical line is string 6, the low E string.

Are any chord fingerings supplied?+

No. These are blank diagrams for your own chord shapes, not a chord dictionary.

How do I print the correct size?+

Download the size that matches your paper. In your PDF viewer, select Actual size / 100% and turn off Fit or Shrink. Measure the 50 mm calibration ruler after printing.
